Essence · Dark ceremonial stage

The stage is the room the stone lights.

Essence has no dark mode. It has one dark surface, used where the product goes quiet and a single object carries the beat: the crystallize, preserved, detail and playback phases of First Breath. Everything here is promoted verbatim from the Step 5 First Playback build — values that were measured, shipped, and argued over, not redrawn for this card.

The stone itself lives in ds/breath-stone.html. This card is the atmosphere around it, the type that survives on ink, and the four rules that cost the build a day each.

The stage, at rest and lit

Six atmosphere layers plus the stone's own seven. Nothing here is a static image of a glow: --lum (fast follower, syllable articulation) and --sus (slow follower, sustained swell) are set by one requestAnimationFrame loop, and every layer reads them through calc() on opacity or transform only. The two frames below are the same markup at --lum: 0 and --lum: .85.

Layer order — build to this

Painted back to front. The four page-level layers sit in a single .atmos element at z-index: 0; l-bloom and l-contact are children of the stone's wrapper so they travel with the object. Content sits at z-index: 1, and the stage sets isolation: isolate so the screen and overlay blends cannot reach the page behind it.

Primary and focus on ink

Mineral #7A8088 is near-invisible on warm ink and appears nowhere on a dark stage. The primary takes the honey fill; the focus ring is the same honey, and it is the one warm value on the stage permitted to sit outside --stone-halo — precisely because it must not track luminance. A focus ring that dims when the voice goes quiet is not a focus ring.

Canon amendment — none needed

Full-width primary on a dark ceremonial stage: confirmed correct.

Canon says a primary is full width on mobile, and a reviewer will keep asking whether a lone 330 px slab is too loud for a reverent screen. It is not, and the reason is worth recording so the next screen doesn't re-litigate it: the dark stage gives the button no container to sit inside. On cream there is a card edge, a gutter, a surface boundary — something for a smaller button to belong to. On ink there is nothing, so width is the only property left that reads as authority. A centred auto-width button on ink reads as a link that gained a background.

Status on ink — the ruling

Status on a ceremonial dark stage is carried by language and position, never colour. The system gets no dark-stage terracotta, sage, or amber-umber.

Thread 2 declined to invent a dark terracotta and carried three non-happy states with copy and placement instead. That was the right instinct for one screen; it is now the rule, for three reasons.

  1. The warm status hues are tuned for cream and only for cream. #9C3528 reads 6.72:1 on #FBF8F4 and roughly 1.9:1 on #1E1B18 — a hole in the screen, not a warning.
  2. Lightening them leaves the palette. Raising terracotta to AA on ink produces a salmon that belongs to no other Essence surface and reads as an alert borrowed from a different app. The warmth that makes the status family cohere on cream is exactly what collapses on ink.
  3. The stage exists to hold one thing at a time. A coloured notice competes with the stone at the moment the stone is the message. Failure that needs colour to be understood is failure that was written badly.

So write it instead. A notice on ink is 15 px Inter at --on-dark-body, in the aside slot — mutually exclusive with the aside, so no state is taller than the happy path. No fill, no border, no icon, no colour. It carries role="status" aria-live="polite", and it follows the house error pattern: what happened, what to do next.

The one escape hatch, and it is a surface change, not a colour. If a dark stage ever must carry a genuinely destructive confirmation — deleting a voice, not a playback that failed — it does so on a cream card (--color-surface-card) laid over the stage, and inside that card the normal warm status colours apply unchanged. The system never needs a status value for ink; it changes what the message is sitting on.

Corollary for reviewers: "this error needs to be red" is, on a dark stage, a note about the sentence. Rewrite the sentence.

Four rules, not four habits

Each of these bit the Step 5 build once, silently — nothing threw, nothing looked broken in a screenshot, and each cost real time to find. They are properties of the medium, not of that screen.

Performance

will-change is a state, not a declaration.

Declare it only under the class that means "this is actually animating right now" — on Step 5, .speaking: zero promoted layers at rest, eight for the ~5 s of the utterance.

The choreography is compositor-bound by design, so main-thread cost was never the exposure — GPU layer memory was. Measured: p95 16.7 ms under a synthetic 6 ms-per-frame main-thread tax, zero frames over 20 ms. Related: keep blend-mode layers at inset: 0. .l-grain at inset: -50% was 1.32 M overlay-blended pixels on a layer that never animates, and cost more than every promoted layer put together.

Reveal choreography

Unmount, don't just fade.

An element at opacity: 0 still occupies its row. On a stage where one action arrives alone and 2.5 s of nothing is the design, an invisible button silently sets the spacing of everything below it.

Sequenced elements mount and unmount; opacity is for the transition, not for the absence. This is also what keeps every non-happy state at or below the happy path's height.

Reveal choreography

Reveal with an animation, not a transition — on anything interactive.

A 1400 ms transform transition for the CTA's rise also governs :active. The press stops being tactile and nobody can say why: the button simply feels dead.

Use @keyframes rise for the entrance so the transition stays free for :active at --duration-small. Any element with both an entrance and an interaction state needs this separation.

Layout

A flex column child does not inherit its parent's width.

.actions needed align-self: stretch before width: 100%; max-width: 330px on the primary would bind. Without it the "full-width" CTA rendered at its old 279 px content width — correct-looking, wrong.

When a percentage width doesn't take, the parent's cross-axis size is the suspect, not the child's declaration.

Ceremonial type

Font-relative measures do not respond to a fit step.

ch scales with the font, so a line rags identically at 34 px and at 26 px: a size clamp can never buy a line back, and a fit loop over font-size is a no-op against a ch measure. Ceremonial display text takes a px measure — Step 5 uses 255px, the width 15ch produced at the tuned size — and then steps the size down until the line rags to at most three lines inside its reserved block.

Two companions to that rule. Size ceremonial type in container units, not vw (clamp(26px, 10.3cqw, 34px)): the same arithmetic, but correct when the screen is a 390 px frame inside a desktop review page. And reserve the block, then prove all candidate lines fit it — the layout must not belong to one sentence. Re-fit on document.fonts.ready, or you have measured the fallback face.
