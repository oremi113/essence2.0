Essence · Step 5

First Playback

The fifth and final phase of the First Breath ceremony. The stone never leaves the screen; it stops breathing and starts speaking. Answers to §4 are below the prototype.

Runs at real timings. The prototype speaks no synthetic audio — a fake voice would mislead the review — so the stone's amplitude and the word reveal are driven by the measured cadence of the real line (3.6 s). Wire the generateSpeech() buffer into an AnalyserNode and feed RMS to setLum(); every timing survives unchanged.

This pass raised it: the card specified a 320 px first-breath hero and ≥48 px clearance on every side, and on a 390 px viewport those are mutually exclusive — 390 − 96 = 294 px maximum. Every phase of the ceremony carried it, not just this one.

Motion — speech is not breath

The idle breath is 5.5% scale, 3 s, symmetric. If speech is also a scale pulse of similar magnitude, the most important state change in the beat is invisible. Speech differs in character:

Two-envelope follower. A fast follower (attack 0.35, release 0.10) drives --lum: syllable articulation. A slow follower (0.018) drives --sus: a sustained 4.5% swell across the whole utterance that decays after. Breath oscillates; speech rises, articulates, and settles.

Everything animated is opacity or transform, driven by two CSS variables from one requestAnimationFrame loop. No layout, no paint on the critical path.

Measured, not assumed. 599 frames unthrottled: p95 13.6 ms, one frame over 20 ms. Under a synthetic 6 ms-per-frame main-thread tax: p95 16.7 ms, zero frames over 20 ms. The choreography is compositor-bound, as designed, so the exposure was never main-thread cost — it was GPU layer memory. will-change was declared statically on nine elements and is now applied only while .speaking is set. .l-grain dropped from inset:-50% (1.32 M px of overlay-blended pixels, on a layer that never animates) to inset:0 (329 k px): the grain alone had been costing more than all nine promoted layers combined. Still owed: a real device at 4× CPU throttle.

Copy — Silent register, Elevated staging

The Copy Guide puts first playback in Silent, and its own model line for this beat — "That's you. Clear, steady, familiar." — carries no ceremony in the language at all. The weight is in the dark, the stone, and the pause. If the words also announce the moment, they compete and the writing loses. No Elevated moment is spent here.

The lede names the 25 prompts. "Moments" is the noun voice training already uses (MOMENT 5 OF 25), so it lands as continuity, not a stat. This is the screen paying off the investment rather than assuming the user remembers it.

Cut entirely: the reassurance line ("nothing was sent / nothing was kept"). "Kept" reads as my recording wasn't saved to someone who just paid to have it kept, and every rewrite of it stayed abstract — sent where, to whom? The screen now ends on recognition. §2's demonstration requirement is carried by the CTA instead: "Create your first message" states that none exists yet, without narrating an absence.

The aside is observational. "It kept the pauses." — the guide's own model line ends "Clear, steady, familiar," three evaluative adjectives; plenty of 60-year-olds hear thin, old, not me and are contradicted at the highest-stakes second of the flow. The slot also hosts three non-happy notices (15 px Inter), so the aside stays 18 px Spectral italic: the family is what tells the moment apart from the product speaking.

CTA: "Create your first message" — house verb from the guide's bank, and "your first" carries the firstness. No pacing microcopy under it: the CTA arriving alone, with nothing else moving, is the no-rush signal.

§4 — answered

4.1 · A fifth phase of First Breath owner

forming → crystallize → preserved → detail → playback. The stone is the voice; making the user watch it disappear and reappear on a new URL breaks exactly the continuity this beat exists to prove. No route, no URL lock.

Un-resumability is a real cost. Mitigation: choose 4.3(c) caching, so a user who closes the tab isn't losing the artifact — only this framing of it. /dev/first-playback renders the phase in isolation regardless.

4.2 · "If you're hearing this, I found a way to stay." owner call

Continuity-framed. Neutral to any recipient, creates no Message, and it's the sentence that survives being played aloud to family in the room — it's addressed to no one and therefore to everyone.

Held to 3.6 s spoken. Two alternates in the same register if it tests soft: "Whatever happens, this much of me stays." / "I'm still here, in the way I could manage."

4.3 · Rendered during processing, cached on the profile owner

Fire on /app/voice/processing the moment vendor_voice_id lands, keyed idempotently on the profile id so a refresh or double-tap can't bill twice. Zero wait at the peak. Cost is one render per activated user.

Persist it — sample_audio_path, sample_duration_ms nullable on voice_profiles — so this is also 4.3(c) and 4.5 is answered for free later.

4.4 · Autoplay, with the silent-phone state designed owner

Autoplay, because a play button turns a gift into a task. The ceremony has had gestures, so the policy permits it — verify on a real iPhone before ship.

The real failure mode isn't autoplay being blocked, it's the phone being on silent: iOS reports the audio as playing while the user hears nothing. Both look identical to the user and want one shared state — deferred to a later pass. The always-visible line means even an undetected silent phone still delivers the beat.

4.6 · Skip is offered, late deferred

"Occur" is satisfied by being offered — never trap someone in a modal to satisfy a spec line. The skip renders only after the line has played and settled, so it's an exit rather than a dismissal. Not prototyped in this pass — happy path first.

4.7 · Failure names the failure, protects the voice deferred

Direction agreed: name what broke, protect the voice ("your voice is preserved and safe — this was only the playback failing"), one retry, always a path onward. Not prototyped in this pass.

4.8 · No waveform — the stone is the waveform owner

A separate waveform would be a fourth playback dialect and the most expensive element on a throttled phone. The stone already breathes with the user's voice while recording; here it breathes with their voice played back. Same grammar, closed loop.

The line is always on screen owner call

Not a caption, not a fallback — typeset at 34 px as the primary visual of the beat, at 13% opacity and 4 px blur before it's spoken, resolving word by word as it plays. It's what a screen reader announces, it's the whole beat for a deaf user, and it's the only thing that makes a muted phone survivable. One sentence closes three states.

Now implemented, not only argued. The word spans are aria-hidden decoration; a role="status" aria-live="polite" region receives the full line at playback and the payoff line at its own beat, so a screen reader gets the ceremony in order and in time. The stone carries role="img". Focus styling is deliberately held for the craft pass.

"Vault" appears zero times

Confirmed across every state. The voice is "your voice" throughout — no "clone", "model", or "sample" on screen.
