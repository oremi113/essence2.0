Essence

A voice is the first thing grief takes. We keep it.

Essence captures the particular sound of a person — how they said your name — and preserves it, so the people they love can still hear them speak. Recorded once, in their own voice, kept for good.

The human truth

Photographs survive. Voices don't.

When someone dies, their photos and videos remain — but the actual sound of them is almost never captured on purpose, and it's the first thing memory loses. Within a year, most people can no longer summon exactly how a parent said goodnight.

Families already reach for this instinctively: saved voicemails replayed until the audio wears thin, old answering machines never erased. The demand is real and universal — there has simply never been a product built to hold it with the care it deserves.

Why now

Three curves crossed at once.

Fidelity. Voice synthesis crossed the believability threshold in the last ~18 months — a few minutes of clean audio now yields a voice a loved one accepts as real, not uncanny.

Cost. What required a studio and a research team is now an API call measured in cents. A premium, human product can finally be built on top of it at consumer margins.

Culture. Digital-legacy planning has gone mainstream, and an aging population is actively looking for meaningful ways to leave something behind. The willingness to pay is arriving at the same moment the technology became good enough to earn trust.

The product

Record once. Preserved for good. Delivered in their voice.

Essence turns voice preservation into a ceremony, not a settings screen. A person is guided through a short, reverent recording, their voice is sealed in a private Vault, and they can leave messages that loved ones receive — spoken in their own voice, on the occasions that matter.

i.

Record

A guided recording ceremony captures enough of a person's voice to preserve it faithfully — designed to feel like a moment, not a chore.

ii.

Preserve

The voice is sealed in the Vault — a private, enduring place that holds who they are and the messages they choose to leave.

iii.

Deliver

Named recipients receive messages in that voice — a birthday reached, a few words for a day the person knew they might miss.

The Vault isn't storage. It's the reason people stay — and the reason they can't leave.

How it makes money · How it defends itself

Business model

Subscription, priced at $12.99/mo (or $119/yr) for the Vault — recurring preservation, storage, and the standing ability to create and deliver messages. People pay to keep something they cannot afford to lose.

The tier ladder climbs with intent: Legacy and Guardian unlock as people leave more messages and preserve more voices — expansion revenue that arrives exactly when attachment is highest.

Why it's defensible

  • Not the model. Synthesis is a commodity we rent. The moat is everything around it.
  • The relationship graph. Recorded voices, chosen recipients, and written messages accrue into something a competitor cannot replicate or export.
  • Trust as a brand. In a category everyone is nervous to get wrong, being the name families trust is itself the barrier to entry.
  • Distribution as a discipline. The real bottleneck isn't building this — it's reaching people who aren't searching, profitably. That's a performance-marketing problem, and it's the founder's decade of expertise (see below).
  • Switching cost is emotional. Your voice and your last words live here. Nobody moves that.
Landscape

Others have circled this. Nobody is building what we're building.

Interest in the space is real — and that's a signal, not a threat. It validates the demand while confirming how early it is. But the field so far splits into three approaches, and none of them is Essence.

Conversational avatars
StoryFile, You Only Virtual. A simulated version of a person to "talk to." Striking in a demo, but leans uncanny and speaks to grief after the fact.
Interactive memory Q&A
HereAfter AI. Record stories; loved ones ask questions later. An archive to query — not messages left, on purpose, for specific people.
Voice-clone utilities
ElevenLabs, Apple Personal Voice. The raw capability, with no legacy product, no delivery, and no trust wrapper around it.

Where Essence sits

Essence is neither a séance nor an archive. It is preparation: a living person preserves their own voice and leaves intentional messages, delivered to the people they choose, at the moments that matter.

  • Prepared, not reactive. Built while someone is alive and themselves — not assembled by the bereaved in grief.
  • Delivered, not queried. Messages reach named recipients at real occasions — it isn't a database you interrogate.
  • Messages, not a simulated conversation with the dead. We deliver what a person actually chose to say — a line we hold on purpose, because it's also where trust lives.
  • A product, not a capability. Reverent design and a trusted brand on top of commodity synthesis anyone can rent.

Go-to-market

This category is won at the moment — not the search.

Nobody wakes up wanting to preserve a voice. There is no search intent to capture and no keyword to bid on — which is exactly why incumbents underestimate it. Essence is acquired the way the best consumer brands are: by reaching people at the emotional moments that make preservation suddenly urgent, with creative good enough to convert a heavy, once-in-a-lifetime decision. That is a performance-marketing problem — and it is the founder's decade of work.

The channel ladder — reach now, trust next, compounding after
Reach
Emotional video creative on Meta and TikTok, aimed at trigger-moment audiences. Immediate, scalable, and the founder's core competency.
Trust
Partnerships where these moments already happen — hospice and palliative care, estate attorneys, senior living, financial advisors. Higher trust, lower CAC, a defensible channel.
Compound
The product seeds its own growth: every message names a recipient who meets Essence at the most resonant moment possible — and gifting turns one account into several.

How it pencils: paid social proves the funnel; partnerships and the built-in recipient loop blend CAC down from there. The model needs a ~$35–45 blended CAC to work — the exact discipline the founder has run against a $2K+ ticket before. Beachhead: we open on adult children (40–55) preserving an aging parent's voice — reachable on Meta, deeply motivated, and able to pay — then widen to the other moments once the funnel is proven.

Where this goes

Voice is the beginning.

We start with the sound of a person because it is the most irreplaceable thing, and the first thing grief takes. But the trust that lets someone preserve their voice is the same trust to hold more of what makes them themselves — their words, their stories, the things they would want said when they can no longer say them.

Photographs became how the world keeps a face. Essence intends to be how it keeps the voice — and everything a voice carries — so that being remembered is never left to chance.
